## Why does gypsum dominate modern construction?

Gypsum dominates because it solves several problems at once: speed, fire safety, acoustics, weight and design freedom. No single competing material delivers all of these together at a comparable cost. Below are the seven advantages that explain its dominance.

### 1. Installation speed

Compared to traditional wet plastering, a gypsum board system installs up to five times faster. A two-person crew can frame, board and tape 20 to 30 square meters of partition in a single working day, and there is no long wet curing period before the next trade can start. In commercial projects where every day of program has a cost, this speed is often the deciding factor.

### 2. Superior acoustic insulation

A single skin of board does little on its own, but a properly built double system with an air cavity and mineral wool inside can reach 50 to 60 dB of airborne sound reduction. That is enough to keep a normal conversation from passing between two hotel rooms or two offices. The acoustic performance depends on the number of board layers, the depth of the cavity and the density of the insulation.

### 3. Fire protection

Gypsum is naturally fire resistant because its core holds chemically bound water. When heated, this water is slowly released as steam, which delays the temperature rise on the protected side. Standard board gives around 30 minutes of resistance, while fire-rated (FR) boards in multiple layers reach 60 to 120 minutes. In airports, shopping centers and stairwells this is not optional; it is written into the building code.

### 4. Thermal insulation

Gypsum systems with mineral wool or other insulation inside the cavity reduce heat transfer through walls and ceilings, which lowers heating and cooling demand. While gypsum is not the primary thermal envelope of a building, lined internal walls and dropped ceilings noticeably improve comfort and energy efficiency in finished interiors.

### 5. Design flexibility

No other finishing material matches gypsum for shaping interiors. It bends into curved walls, steps into multi-level ceilings, forms bulkheads and coves, and hides lighting, ventilation ducts, sprinklers and cabling above and within it. This is why architects specify it for lobbies, restaurants and feature ceilings where the form has to follow a precise design.

### 6. Structural lightness

A gypsum partition weighs roughly 25 kg per square meter, against 150 to 200 kg for a brick wall and 250 to 350 kg for cast concrete. Lighter internal walls mean smaller loads on slabs and foundations, which can reduce structural cost across a whole building and make later layout changes far easier.

### 7. Cost effectiveness

When you add up material, labor, time on site and the quality of the finished surface, gypsum delivers the lowest cost per square meter of completed interior. It also produces a flat, paint-ready surface, which shortens the [painting](/en/painting/) stage that follows.

## What are the main types of gypsum board?

Choosing the right board for each room is what separates a durable installation from one that fails early. The table below summarizes the common board types, where they belong and the indicative facts you can rely on when planning.

| Board type | Best application | Key fact |
|------------|------------------|----------|
| Standard (GKB) | Living rooms, bedrooms, dry ceilings | Around 30 minutes fire resistance |
| Moisture resistant (GKBI) | Bathrooms, kitchens, laundry | Treated core resists humidity, often green faced |
| Fire resistant (GKF) | Stairwells, corridors, plant rooms | 60 to 120 minutes resistance in multiple layers |
| Acoustic | Studios, meeting rooms, cinemas | High density core for 50 to 60 dB reduction |
| Impact resistant | Schools, corridors, public areas | Reinforced core for high-traffic zones |
| Aquapanel (cement board) | Facades, wet rooms, exterior soffits | Cement based, not gypsum, fully water tolerant |

Using a moisture-resistant board in a bathroom and a fire-rated board in an evacuation corridor is not a style choice; it is what the specification and the building code require. We match every board to its location during the on-site survey so the system performs as intended for its full service life.

## How is a gypsum system actually built?

A standard partition starts with a metal track fixed to the floor and ceiling, then vertical studs spaced at 40 or 60 centimeters. Mineral wool is placed inside the cavity for acoustic and thermal performance, and services such as cables and pipes run within the same void. Board is then screwed to both faces, joints are taped and filled, and the surface is sanded smooth.

Coordination with other trades matters here. Electrical conduit, sockets and lighting boxes have to be set before the second face is closed, which is why we plan this carefully with electricians; you can read more in our guide on [coordinating gypsum with electrical installations](/en/blog/coordinating-gypsum-with-electrical-installations/). Suspended ceilings follow a similar logic, hanging a metal grid from the slab and leaving an accessible void for mechanical and electrical equipment.

## How does gypsum compare to plaster and brick?

Traditional brick-and-plaster construction is solid and familiar, but it is slow and heavy. A plastered brick wall needs days to dry before painting, adds significant weight, and is difficult to modify once built. Gypsum reverses all three drawbacks: it is dry-built, light and demountable.

That said, the two approaches are not enemies. In many of our projects, structural and party walls stay in masonry while internal layout walls, linings and ceilings are gypsum. Where existing masonry needs a smooth finish, [professional plastering](/en/blog/benefits-professional-plastering/) bridges the two systems and prepares the surface for paint. The right combination depends on the building, which is why a site visit always comes first.

In commercial buildings, gypsum carries the fire and acoustic requirements that keep occupants safe and comfortable, while letting designers shape lobbies, conference rooms and retail interiors. In homes, the same material creates feature ceilings, TV walls, niches and clean partition layouts without the mess and weight of masonry. ## What mistakes cause gypsum installations to fail early?

Most gypsum problems are not faults of the material but of how it was fitted. The recurring mistakes we are called to repair are predictable, and avoiding them costs nothing extra at the time of installation.

- Using standard board in wet rooms. A normal GKB board in a bathroom or kitchen absorbs moisture, swells and eventually crumbles. Moisture-resistant board is required wherever splashing or steam is regular.
- Spacing studs too widely. Studs set beyond 60 centimeters leave the board flexing under pressure, which cracks the joints. Heavy-traffic walls in schools or corridors should drop to 40 centimeters.
- Skipping mineral wool in the cavity. Leaving the void empty halves the acoustic performance, so a wall that should reach 50 dB barely manages 30.
- Fixing into the board instead of the structure. Hanging a television or cabinet on board alone will tear it out. Loads must reach a stud, a reinforcing batten or a dedicated fixing plate set during framing.
- Painting joints before they fully dry. Filler that is sealed under paint too soon shrinks and shows a line along every seam.

We plan around these points on site, which is why our installations hold their finish for years rather than months.

## How do you maintain and repair gypsum surfaces?

Gypsum needs almost no routine maintenance, but minor damage is normal in any busy interior and is simple to put right. Small dents and screw pops are filled with jointing compound, sanded flat once dry, and spot-painted; the repaired patch is invisible after a fresh coat. A clean cut-out and a board patch fix larger holes, and because the system is dry-built, a single bay can be opened and reclosed without disturbing the rest of the wall.

Drying time matters at every stage. Jointing compound typically needs 24 hours per coat before sanding, and a freshly filled wall should rest a day before painting so the surface is stable. For everyday cleaning, a painted gypsum wall wipes down with a damp cloth; in kitchens and bathrooms a washable finish makes this far easier and protects the board behind it.
